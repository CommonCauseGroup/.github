# Code of conduct

## The short version

People come here to build software for people having a hard time. Behave like
somebody who means that.

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Common Cause — as a contributor, a maintainer, or
somebody who has opened one issue and will never return — to be free of
harassment for everyone, whatever their age, body, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, appearance,
race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We are a mental health organisation, and we say the obvious thing plainly:
**many of the people in these repositories have their own experience of mental
illness, including maintainers.** That is not a disclosure anybody owes anyone,
and it is not a thing to be handled delicately. It is a reason the ordinary
rules matter more here than they might elsewhere.

## What we expect

- **Assume the other person is trying.** A confusing issue is usually somebody doing their best in a second language, on a phone, at the end of a long day.
- **Be direct about the work and gentle about the person.** "This will drop the foreign key — here is why" is a good review. "Did you even test this?" is not, and it does not become one by being correct.
- **Take the disagreement to the merits.** Decisions here are made by argument, not by seniority or persistence.
- **Accept a no.** Maintainers turn down changes that are perfectly good code; that is most of the job. Re-litigating a decided question in a new issue is not persistence, it is noise.
- **Say when you were wrong.** Cheaply, once, and then move on. Nobody here needs an apology longer than the mistake.
- **Respect what people choose to share.** Someone mentioning their own diagnosis in an issue has told you one thing about themselves, not given you a topic.

## What is not acceptable

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwanted sexual attention of any kind.
- Insults, personal or political attacks, and trolling.
- Harassment, public or private, including sustained unwelcome contact after being asked to stop.
- Publishing anyone's private information — address, email, workplace, medical history — without their explicit permission.
- **Posting real people's data in an issue, a pull request or a screenshot.** This is harm, not carelessness, whether or not it was meant that way. Redact first.
- **Diagnosing, advising or debating a named individual's mental health**, including a contributor's, including approvingly, including in a joke.
- **Using our issue tracker as a crisis line, or answering somebody as though it were one.** If you are in crisis, please see the routes below — they are staffed and this is not. If you see somebody in distress in a thread, do not attempt to counsel them: point them at those routes and tell a maintainer.
- Anything else which would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## If you need help right now

Nobody is watching this repository for a crisis, and everything filed here is
public and permanent.

If you are in immediate danger, call **999** (UK) or your local emergency
number. To talk to somebody at any hour, **Samaritans** are on **116 123**, free,
from any phone. Outside the UK, [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com)
will find the equivalent where you are.

## Scope

This applies in every space the organisation runs — repositories, issues, pull
requests, discussions, and any chat or event under the Common Cause name — and
whenever somebody is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report abuse, harassment or anything else unacceptable to
**conduct@\<our domain\>**. Reports go to a small group and are held in
confidence; we will not share your identity with the person reported without
asking you first.

Tell us what happened, where, and roughly when — links help enormously. You do
not have to be the person it happened to, and you do not have to be certain it
counts. If you are not sure, report it and let us decide; that is our job, not
yours.

**If the report is about a maintainer**, say so and send it anyway. It goes to
the same address and that person is removed from handling it.

We will acknowledge a report within **three working days** and tell you the
outcome once there is one.

## What happens next

Maintainers are responsible for enforcing this, and may edit or remove comments,
commits, code, issues and any other contribution that breaks it, giving reasons
when they do.

Depending on what happened and whether it has happened before, the response is
one of:

1. **A private word** — what was wrong, and what to do instead. Most things end here.
2. **A warning** — a stated consequence for continuing, and no interaction with the people involved for a set period.
3. **A temporary ban** from all public and private interaction with the project.
4. **A permanent ban.**

We will move straight to the last of these for harassment, for aggression toward
an individual, and for publishing somebody's private information.
